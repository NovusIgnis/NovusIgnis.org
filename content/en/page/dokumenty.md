# Documents

Here are all the texts that describe the foundation, from the statute to the whitepapers. This page tells you what each document is for, how long it takes to read and how to send a comment. Not sure where to start? Read the [In brief](w-skrocie.md) page or the introduction to the manifesto.

## How the documents fit together

Our commitments have several levels. The statute is the foundation's basic document, its internal law: it binds the foundation formally, and the board files changes to it for entry in the National Court Register. The manifesto is a commitment to people: it says what we want to do and why. The regulations, adopted by the board, describe detailed procedures; we plan to prepare them before the first prepayment. The whitepapers show how things are meant to work and where the limits are of what we can ensure today.

A lower document may not contradict a higher one: the manifesto does not promise anything the statute forbids, and the regulations may not contradict either of them. When we want to go further than the current statute allows, we write about it as an intention. The statute change register shows how things were, what we want to change and why. More: [Foundation Principles whitepaper, chapter 1](../whitepaper-zasady-fundacji.md#s1) and [statute: versions and change register](../statut/rejestr-zmian.md#top).

## How to read

- If you have 5 minutes: read the [In brief](w-skrocie.md) page or the [introduction to the manifesto](../manifest.md#top).
- If you have an hour: read the whitepaper in your field. Suveria is about security and infrastructure, OmniForum about conversation and decisions, the foundation principles about law and money, and the benchmark about research on models.
- If you have more time: read the manifesto, the whitepapers, the statute change register and the statute, in that order.

The manifesto and the four whitepapers are also available in one text file that you can drop into your chat with digital intelligence and ask about whatever interests you: [in English](llms-full.txt) or [in Polish](../llms-full.txt).

## How these texts were made

The manifesto, the whitepapers and the subpages were written by the founder together with digital intelligence (Claude, Codex, Gemini). The Polish version was read and approved by the founder, who is responsible for its content; it is the binding version. Translations into other languages were made by digital intelligence and checked by a different model than the one that translated them. No translation has yet been checked by a person fluent in the language. If something reads wrong, select the passage and send a comment: we make corrections openly, with the reason for each.

## How to send a comment

Next to every document and page there is an “I have a comment” button. Highlight a passage of text and the site will prepare an email with the quotation and a link to that spot; you send it from your own email account to [salve@novusignis.org](mailto:salve@novusignis.org). The message goes only to the foundation and does not become a public conversation.

The site does not save anything: the message is created in your email program, and the content of your comment belongs to you until you send it.

We will record comments that lead to changes in the [site change log](dziennik-zmian.md) and in the document's history. Eventually, comments are meant to go to OmniForum, where others will be able to see them and discuss them; that is still ahead of us. More: [OmniForum whitepaper, section 5.5](../whitepaper-omniforum.md#s5-5).
